# Raw survey 4 — Open geospatial data sources for a frozen "world snapshot": Paris, London, NYC (+ candidate cities)

Collected 11 Sep 2026. Method: WebSearch + portal page fetches. Purpose: identify layers that can be **bulk-downloaded once and frozen locally**, so a tool-using VLM agent can answer verifiable spatial questions ("cafés within 300 m of the station open after 20:00", "which side of this street gets afternoon sun", "how noisy is this spot", "nearest frequent-service transit stop") with no live API in the training loop.

**Honesty flags.** Entries marked *(unverified)* were not confirmed against a portal page in this pass — treat as leads, not facts. Licence strings are as stated by the publisher where checked; where a portal states only "open data" without a named licence I say so. Nothing below is invented, but portal URLs and dataset slugs drift, so re-check before building an ingestion script.

## Paris

Paris is, for this task, the strongest of the three: it is the only one of the three with **open sidewalk-surface polygons**, a **solar/shadow cadastre computed from a real 3D model**, a **triennial door-to-door retail census**, and a **national 50 cm LiDAR DTM/DSM programme** — all under Licence Ouverte or ODbL.

Two portals do most of the work, and both have good machine access:
- **Paris Data** (`opendata.paris.fr`) — Opendatasoft. Every dataset has: CSV/GeoJSON/Shapefile/GeoPackage export, an **Explore API v2.1** supporting `where=within_bbox(geo_point_2d, lat1,lon1,lat2,lon2)` and `within_distance(...)`, **and a site-wide WFS** at `https://opendata.paris.fr/api/wfs?SERVICE=WFS&REQUEST=GetCapabilities` (so `BBOX=` clipping works out of the box, e.g. via `ogr2ogr -spat`). Default licence **ODbL**.
- **APUR Open Data** (`opendata.apur.org`) — ArcGIS Hub. Shapefile/GeoJSON/File-GDB download, **ArcGIS REST FeatureServer with `geometry=<bbox>&geometryType=esriGeometryEnvelope`**, so bbox clipping is native.
- Plus **IGN** (`geoservices.ign.fr` / `cartes.gouv.fr`) for national base data, all **Licence Ouverte / Etalab 2.0** since 1 Jan 2021.

### Quality and gaps assessment — Paris

- **Best-in-class for this project.** Paris is the only city of the three that gives you, all open and all clippable: sidewalk *surface polygons*, a tree inventory with heights and species, a shadow-aware solar cadastre, modelled noise isophones, a 10 m air-quality field, a field-surveyed retail census, geolocated terrace permits, and 50 cm LiDAR. That combination supports every one of the target question types with real, checkable ground truth.
- **Licence situation is clean**: ODbL (Ville de Paris), Licence Ouverte/Etalab 2.0 (IGN, Airparif, Bruitparif), APUR open data. All permit redistribution with attribution; ODbL share-alike applies to derived *databases*, which matters if you publish the generated QA corpus — **flag this in the README and attribute properly**. This is a real, not theoretical, obligation for an ODbL-derived training set.
- **Opening hours for *commercial* POIs is the weakest link**, as it is everywhere. BDCOM has no hours; SIRENE has no hours; OSM is the only broad source and is incomplete. **But** the *Îlots de fraîcheur* datasets give authoritative, daily-synced hours for parks, pools, libraries, museums and churches — so the "what's open at time T" question class has a verifiable backbone. Recommended split: verifiable hours from municipal facilities, explicitly-synthetic hours for shops/cafés, and say which is which in the README.
- **No open pedestrian-count network.** Cycle, multimodal and road counts are excellent; pedestrians are absent.
- **No free official LOD2 CityGML.** You will extrude footprints. For your use case (shadows, visibility, "how tall") LOD1 from APUR heights is adequate and arguably *better* because it's simple and reproducible; say so rather than apologising for it.
- **Coordinate-system trap**: the Plan de Voirie originates in Lambert I-54 (a Ville-de-Paris local system) and has been reprojected more than once. Verify against a known landmark on ingest rather than trusting the declared CRS.
- **Freshness mismatch**: BDCOM is triennial (2023 latest), noise maps are 5-yearly, LiDAR HD is a rolling programme, GTFS refreshes 3×/day. A "world snapshot" is inherently a composite of different vintages — **record the vintage of every layer in the snapshot manifest**, because a question like "is this café still there" is only well-posed relative to a stated date.

### Quality and gaps assessment — London

- **Strong overall, with one world-class layer.** The TfL Cycling Infrastructure Database — every street in all 33 boroughs physically surveyed, ~240k assets, 480k photographs, open licence, bulk download — has no equivalent in Paris or NYC. Combined with EA 1 m LiDAR, Defra 10 m noise, LAEI 20 m air quality, 1.14M located street trees and dense central OSM, a few-km² London snapshot is genuinely rich.
- **Sidewalk widths are the worst gap, and worse than it first looks.** The only verified open width dataset covers **only the City of London (~2.9 km²)** and its publisher warns it may be stale. Outside the Square Mile there is no London-wide open footway-width layer at all, OSM `width=*` on footways is rare, and sidewalk tagging is inconsistent between boroughs. **Pragmatic consequence: if pavement width matters, pick the City of London as the district** — that single choice converts the worst gap into a solved layer, and the City is conveniently exactly district-sized.
- **OS licensing is the structural problem, and it bites precisely where the data is best.** MasterMap Topography, the Building Height Attribute, and OS NGD (signed *and* observed speeds for every GB road) are all PSGA/commercial and cannot be redistributed. **Recommendation: build entirely on OGL + ODbL + Overture, and derive building heights from LiDAR DSM − DTM.** Licence-clean, and a stronger interview answer than relying on a privileged data grant.
- **Speed limits are effectively OSM-only** for an open project, with known incompleteness on residential and unclassified streets.
- **Pedestrian footfall is a real hole.** The current, rich data (BT mobility footfall, Mastercard spend, LDC premises/vacancy via the GLA High Streets Data Service, City of London Destination City) is **subscriber-only to boroughs and BIDs**. The only substantial open series, LDC SmartStreetSensor, ends September 2020.
- **Pavement café licences are not available as data** — statutory registers exist but only as searchable web forms. Substitute FHRS + OSM `outdoor_seating`. A direct and instructive contrast with Paris, where the same information is a clean ODbL GeoJSON of ~19,000 permits.
- **Two layers are stale rather than missing**: the London Solar Opportunity Map (2017 footprints, 2006–2016 LiDAR, no planned updates) and Colouring London's building ages (volunteer, patchy, footprints currently excluded from extracts).
- **No open semantic 3D city model.** You will build LoD1 from footprints + DSM yourself.
- **Daytime vs residential population is a modelling trap in central London.** Census OAs give excellent residential resolution, but a Square-Mile-type district has near-zero residential population and a weekday population in the hundreds of thousands.
- **Explicitly unverified**: LAEI 2022 concentration-grid availability; whether TfL publishes a native first-party GTFS; Emu Analytics licence terms; Breathe London and LAQN redistribution terms; Colouring Britain's current extract inventory; GEODS access conditions; OS Data Hub tile granularity per product.
- **Offline feasibility: yes.** Natively bbox-clippable: BBBike, Overture, EA LiDAR and aerial (AOI to 5 km tiles), Defra noise (AOI + WCS), ONS boundaries (WFS), TfL GIS FeatureServers, City of London WFS, Mapillary. Bulk-only then clip locally: Geofabrik, all OS OpenData, LSOM, street trees, DfT counts, VOA, planning.data.gov.uk, NaPTAN, FHRS, BODS. **Nothing essential is API-only** — the only one-time live harvests are TfL Unified API stop/route detail, Breathe London/LAQN, and Mapillary.

## New York City

Two access mechanisms recur and are worth stating once:

- **Socrata / SODA** (NYC Open Data at `data.cityofnewyork.us`; NY State at `data.ny.gov`). Every dataset has a 4-4 identifier. Full export: `https://data.cityofnewyork.us/api/views/<id>/rows.csv?accessType=DOWNLOAD` (also `.geojson` for map datasets); metadata at `/api/views/<id>.json`. **Bbox clipping works** via SoQL `within_box(the_geom, nw_lat, nw_lon, se_lat, se_lon)` on `/resource/<id>.geojson`. - **ArcGIS REST FeatureServer** (NYC OTI org at `services6.arcgis.com/yG5s3afENB5iO9fj/...`, plus DCP orgs). Supports `query?geometry=<xmin,ymin,xmax,ymax>&geometryType=esriGeometryEnvelope&inSR=4326&f=geojson` — **server-side bbox**, with `resultOffset` paging.

**Licence, city-wide — read this before writing a README.** NYC Open Data has **no formal open licence** (no CC, no ODbL). It is governed by the *NYC Open Data Terms of Use* plus the NYC.gov Terms of Use; the portal states there are "no restrictions on the use of Open Data" but disclaims warranty. Practically: free to use and redistribute, attribution polite — **but say "NYC Open Data Terms of Use", not "public domain"**. https://opendata.cityofnewyork.us/faq/

### Quality and gaps assessment — New York City

**NYC is the best-instrumented city in the Americas for this task.** Citywide 6-inch photogrammetric planimetrics, per-building heights, a 70-field lot-attribute table, and 1-ft topobathymetric LiDAR have no US peer. The honest weaknesses:

- **Noise is the single weakest layer, and it is a hard gap.** No strategic noise map, no measured Lden surface, no verifiable DOT or DEP noise dataset. 311 complaints are a *complaint-propensity* proxy with severe demographic confounding — reporting rates vary by neighbourhood income and 311 familiarity far more than sound levels do. **If you use it, label it as such; do not call it "noise".** Paris and London both win decisively here because the EU Environmental Noise Directive forces modelled dB maps to exist.
- **Opening hours: effectively missing, and worse than Paris.** No city dataset publishes them; OSM is the only source and is sparse and chain-biased. NYC has no analogue of Paris's municipal-facility hours feed. If temporal reasoning is central to the task design, this bottlenecks it — synthesise from POI category priors and *document that they are synthetic*, or drop the question class.
- **Sidewalk widths are not published — only polygons.** Width must be derived (medial axis / distance transform between curb line and building line). Doable and defensible, but it is *your computation*, not city data, and it will be wrong at corners, plazas and under scaffolding. The sidewalk layer last refreshed ~Apr 2024 and **models no obstructions** — scaffolding sheds, bus shelters, tree pits, newsstands, café setups — which dominate real effective width in Manhattan. **Paris's trottoir emprises are the better layer.**
- **Curb regulations are not officially open.** The only structured product found is third-party with unverified licence. NYC publishes no CurbLR or OMF-CDS feed, so parking/loading reasoning is closed off unless you parse the raw parking-sign inventory yourself.
- **Vintage mismatch across the 3D stack** — footprints updated daily, 3D geometry from **2014**, DSM from **2017**, the citywide 1-ft DEM on Open Data from **2010**, land cover 2017, canopy 2021. Any district with post-2014 development (much of Brooklyn, LIC, Hudson Yards) will have footprints with no matching 3D form. **Pick a district with a stable built environment, or document the inconsistency.**
- **No verified post-2017 city LiDAR.** Treat "newer than 2017" as unverified until you check the 3DEP index for your bbox.
- **Flow data is too coarse for a district.** Pedestrian counts exist at 114 locations citywide; traffic speeds cover arterials only; TLC trips aggregate to 263 taxi zones. **Citi Bike trip history is the only genuinely district-resolution flow dataset — and its licence is restrictive.**
- **Air quality is 300 m modelled, not measured**, and EPA monitor density at district scale is effectively zero. Fine for a coarse gradient, wrong for anything block-level.
- **Sun/shadow must be computed, not downloaded.** Arguably a *feature* for an RLVR project — a self-computed shadow field from the DSM is genuinely verifiable — but it is work, and its accuracy inherits the 2014/2017 geometry vintage.
- **Licensing is messier than it looks and needs deliberate design, not an afterthought.** NYC Open Data has **no formal open licence** (ToU only). Orthoimagery is CC BY 4.0. Mapillary is CC BY-**SA** (copyleft — risky to bake into a published dataset). OSM and Overture base/transportation are ODbL (share-alike on derivative *databases*). Citi Bike has a bespoke restrictive licence. **The cleanest fully-permissive spine for a published HF dataset: NYC ToU layers + Overture Places (CDLA-Permissive) + orthos (CC BY) + Census (public domain), keeping ODbL and CC BY-SA sources out of any redistributed derivative.**
- **Unverified items, for completeness**: DCP Building Elevation and Subgrade (format/cadence), NYSERDA solar dataset identity, NYC Ferry landing geometry as a standalone dataset, NAIP's latest NY year, the 2024 LiDAR's NYC coverage, OpenCurb's licence, and the HISTORICAL Sidewalk Café file's bbox-filterability.

### 1. Vienna — the single best fit, and it is not close

Vienna is the only candidate verified to have **all four hard layers at once**: citywide sidewalk *polygons*, a real solar cadastre computed from a 0.5 m ALS surface model with far-field shadowing, a tree cadastre, and a rectangle-based bulk downloader that hands you exactly the district you asked for. The **Flächen-Mehrzweckkarte** is the killer feature — a wall-to-wall area partition of the whole city where every polygon carries a land-use class, and classes 22 / 23 / 24 separate carriageway from sidewalk/footpath from pedestrian zone. ### 4. Helsinki — best *semantic* 3D, uniform CC BY, smallest licence-audit burden

Helsinki's differentiator is that its 3D model is **semantic, not just geometric**: a CityGML city information model with terrain plus buildings as proper CityGML objects in both LoD1 and LoD2, distributed as **4 km² map sheets** — a natural district-sized clipping unit for exactly this project. Alongside sits a **7.5 cm reality mesh** from 42,000+ aerial photos (relevant if you ever want the VLM to look at photoreal renders of the world it reasons about — for a *vision*-language model that is not a side benefit), open city LiDAR, and an HSY per-building photovoltaic potential dataset with a **documented, reproducible suitability rule** (>847 kWh/m²/yr, ≥5 m² contiguous, >0.5 m from roof edge). A published rule like that is gold for generating verifiable questions, because you can re-derive the answer rather than trust it. Everything on HRI, including HSL's GTFS, is CC BY 4.0 — one licence for the whole snapshot. Caveat: hri.fi returned 403 to the fetcher, so the dataset pages below come from search results rather than direct verification, and **sidewalk-class coverage in the city base map is unverified**.

- **3D models of Helsinki — CityGML** — LoD1 + LoD2 semantic buildings + terrain, 4 km² sheets. CC BY 4.0. https://hri.fi/data/en_GB/dataset/helsingin-3d-kaupunkimalli · viewer https://kartta.hel.fi/3d/
- **Lidar datasets of the city of Helsinki** — point clouds, whole city. CC BY 4.0. https://hri.fi/data/en_GB/dataset/helsingin-laserkeilausaineistot
- **Photovoltaic potential in the Helsinki metropolitan area** — per-building PV production potential. https://hri.fi/data/en_GB/dataset/paakaupunkiseudun-aurinkosahkopotentiaali
- **Suitable areas for solar panels** (roof-section geometry meeting the irradiance rule) — https://hri.fi/data/en_GB/dataset/aurinkopaneeleille-sopivat-sijainnit-paakaupunkiseudulla
- **HSL open data / GTFS** — CC BY 4.0. https://www.hsl.fi/en/hsl/open-data

### 5. Melbourne — include it *only* for the footfall layer, which nothing else can replace

Melbourne is not a balanced pick and should not carry the main snapshot. But the **Pedestrian Counting System** is unique: hourly, per-sensor, directional pedestrian counts from ~99 active sensor locations, continuous since 2009, still expanding and still updating monthly in 2026 (312.9 M movements recorded across 2025). If you want genuinely temporal-spatial questions — "is this corner busier at 18:00 on a Thursday than at noon on a Sunday", "which of these two streets carries more foot traffic after dark" — with ground truth that is **measured rather than modelled**, this is the only open dataset in the world at this resolution and duration. It is the direct answer to the footfall gap that Paris, London and NYC all share. The Urban Forest tree inventory (species, dimensions, useful life expectancy per tree) is likewise unusually rich. Everything else is thin: no noise map (Australia has no EU-END equivalent), no open solar cadastre found, weak sidewalk geometry. **Treat Melbourne as a second, specialised world that adds a footfall question family, not as the primary district.**

- **Pedestrian Counting System (counts per hour)** — hourly per-sensor since 2009; CSV/JSON/GeoJSON/SHP via Opendatasoft API (**bbox- and time-filterable**). CC BY 4.0. https://data.melbourne.vic.gov.au/explore/dataset/pedestrian-counting-system-monthly-counts-per-hour/
- **Pedestrian Counting System — Sensor Locations** — https://data.melbourne.vic.gov.au/explore/dataset/pedestrian-counting-system-sensor-locations/
- **Trees, with species and dimensions (Urban Forest)** — 80k+ trees with species, dimensions, life expectancy. CSV/JSON/GeoJSON/KML/SHP/XLS. https://data.melbourne.vic.gov.au/explore/dataset/trees-with-species-and-dimensions-urban-forest/

### Also-rans, and why they fell short

- **Berlin** — the closest near-miss, and worth a second look if licence purity matters most: the Umweltatlas is arguably the best single environmental data collection in Europe (noise, solar potential, heat, ground cover) and everything is **DL-DE/Zero-2.0**, i.e. effectively public domain with **no attribution obligation at all** — the most permissive licence in this entire report. It loses on currency and ergonomics: the LOD2 CityGML model's base capture is 2007–2009 with a 2014 update, no sidewalk-polygon layer could be verified, no open footfall, and FIS-Broker is a painful way to do bbox clipping next to Vienna's rectangle tool or PDOK's OGC API.
- **Copenhagen / Denmark** — the national LiDAR (DHM, ~4.5 pts/m², classified, 1x1 km LAZ tiles) is excellent and free, and Open Data DK has good tree registers. But no national LOD2 building model, no solar cadastre, no sidewalk polygons, no footfall — and the Datafordeler download path is being restructured (raster/point-cloud *Filudtræk* discontinued April 2026, *Fildownload* replacing it), exactly the kind of churn you do not want under a frozen snapshot.
- **Singapore** — disqualified on the decisive layer. The licence is genuinely good (Singapore Open Data Licence, updated Jan 2026, commercial use allowed) and data.gov.sg / OneMap are clean, but building footprints on data.gov.sg come from the URA Master Plan and are explicitly **indicative and incomplete**, there is no open 3D city model, and the well-known open Singapore 3D datasets are academic reconstructions (NUS Urban Analytics Lab; OSM footprints extruded by HDB storey counts) rather than authoritative. A spatial-reasoning ground truth built on that is not verifiable.
- **San Francisco** — strong on the American layers: sidewalk polygons and curb ramps, footprints with LiDAR-derived heights, a detailed street-tree list, USGS 3DEP, excellent GTFS, all effectively public domain. It fails on the *European* layers that make the interesting questions possible — no strategic noise map, no open solar/shadow cadastre. Note DataSF migrated from `data.sfgov.org` to `data.sf.gov`, so old URLs need checking.
- **Chicago** — same shape as SF but thinner: sidewalks and footprints with storey counts exist, but no citywide tree inventory found, no noise, no solar, no footfall.
- **Madrid** — the Mapa Estratégico de Ruido 2021 is real and good, and CNIG provides national LiDAR, but no open 3D building model or solar cadastre at city level, and the portal is fragmented across datos.madrid.es, geoportal.madrid.es and CNIG with inconsistent licensing.
- **Montreal** — a solid public-tree dataset and the Géobase road network, but Géobase is a *centreline* network with no sidewalk surfaces, no 3D building model found, no noise or solar cadastre, and the metropolitan LiDAR sits under a separate CMM licence from the city's own.
- **Lisbon** — none of the layers that matter (3D buildings, LiDAR, sidewalk geometry, solar, noise) could be verified as bulk-downloadable open data. Marked **unverified** rather than absent, but on current evidence it does not belong in the shortlist.

### Recommendations

1. **Paris is the strongest of the three in scope**, and the only one where sidewalk polygons, a shadow-aware solar cadastre, modelled noise, a 10 m air-quality field, a door-to-door retail census, geolocated terrace permits and tree heights all coexist under clean open licences. If you build one city, build Paris.
2. **If you want a second, contrasting city, take Vienna rather than London or NYC.** Vienna matches Paris on sidewalks and solar, beats it on licence uniformity (one CC BY 4.0 string for everything) and on bbox ergonomics (draw a rectangle, get every layer), and has a real LOD2 roof model. It is also a good portfolio signal: it shows the pipeline was designed to be city-agnostic rather than hand-fitted to one dataset.
3. **NYC's honest role is as the anglophone showcase** — MapPLUTO's 70 attribute fields and the 1-ft topobathy LiDAR are genuinely impressive — but its noise gap is unfixable and its licence stack (ToU + CC-BY + ODbL + CC-BY-SA + a bespoke Citi Bike agreement) is the messiest of the three.
4. **London's decisive choice is the district, not the city**: pick the **City of London** and the pavement-width gap disappears; pick anywhere else and it is the worst gap in the report.
5. **Add Melbourne only if measured footfall is a question class you want.** It is the only city on earth with hourly per-sensor pedestrian counts since 2009, and no amount of work on the other cities substitutes for it.
6. **Compute sun/shadow yourself in every city.** No city publishes a time-varying street-level shadow layer; all publish, at best, annual roof-level insolation. Extruding footprints by height and casting shadows with a solar-position library gives you a fully deterministic, re-derivable ground truth — which is exactly what an RLVR reward needs, and a better story than trusting a downloaded number you cannot audit.
7. **Design the licence composition deliberately, before ingesting anything.** ODbL (OSM, Overture base/buildings/transportation, Ville de Paris, Colouring London) carries **share-alike on derivative databases**, and a generated QA corpus derived from them is plausibly a derivative database. CC BY-SA (Mapillary) is copyleft on images. Citi Bike and VOA have bespoke restrictive terms. The cleanest permissive spine is: official municipal layers under CC BY / Licence Ouverte / OGL, **Overture `places` (CDLA-Permissive-2.0, contains no OSM data)**, national mapping agency open data (IGN, swisstopo, PDOK), and census/public-domain sources — with ODbL layers used for *building* the world but their share-alike obligation honoured (or the derived corpus published under ODbL, which is a perfectly respectable choice as long as it is stated).
8. **Record a per-layer vintage manifest.** Every snapshot is a composite of different capture dates — BDCOM triennial, noise 5-yearly, LiDAR rolling, GTFS 3x daily, NYC 3D geometry frozen at 2014. A question like "is this café still there" or "how tall is this building" is only well-posed relative to a stated date. Making the manifest a first-class artifact is cheap, defensible in interview, and pre-empts the obvious reviewer objection.
